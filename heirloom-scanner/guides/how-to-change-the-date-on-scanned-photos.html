How to change the date on scanned photos

Scan a print from 1978 and your iPhone files it under today's date, because as far as the phone is concerned, the photo was created the moment you scanned it. The result is a library where Grandma's wedding sits between this morning's coffee and yesterday's screenshot. Here's why it happens, how to fix it, and how to work out the date of a print when nobody wrote it down.

Why scanned photos show the wrong date

Photos sorts your library by the date stored inside each file, called its metadata. For a picture, that's the "date taken", with the file's creation date as a fallback. A scan, or a photo of a print, is a brand-new file created today, so it carries today's date. It has no way of knowing the print was taken in 1978 unless somebody tells it.

Change the date in the Photos app

  1. Open the photo in the Photos app and tap the info button (the small i), or swipe up on the photo.
  2. Tap Adjust next to the date and time.
  3. Choose the right date and tap Adjust again to save it.

You can also tap the More button (•••) on a photo, then Adjust Date & Time (on iOS 27 it sits under Adjust Info). To move several photos at once, tap Select, choose the photos, and use the same ••• menu. If you only know the year, picking the same date for a whole batch keeps them together in the right year. Apple describes the steps in its iPhone User Guide.

How to date an undated print

  • Turn it over. Look for handwriting, a photo-lab stamp, or a month and year printed on the back or the border. Many prints carried a processing date.
  • Check the neighbors. Albums are usually in order. A dated print on the same page narrows the range for the ones around it.
  • Read the picture. Clothes, cars, hairstyles, a calendar on the wall or a newspaper in the shot all offer clues. If you know when someone was born, their age helps.
  • Ask. Relatives often remember "that was the summer we moved", which is a good enough anchor.

An approximate date is far better than none. It keeps the photo near the right time in your library, and you can refine it later.

When you only know the year or decade

You'll rarely know the exact day. Pick a consistent placeholder, such as the middle of the year, and put the uncertainty in the caption (for example "c. 1978") so you remember it's approximate. If all you know is a season or the decade, record that and note it in the caption.

Add captions while you're at it

People, places and the occasion are the first things to be forgotten. Write who's in the picture, left to right, while someone who knows is still around to ask. A caption takes ten seconds now and is impossible to recover later.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting until everything is scanned. Dating each print as you scan it is much quicker than trying to sort hundreds of undated images later.
  • Misreading the date format. "12/11/65" is December 11 in the US and 12 November in much of the world. Use context to decide.
  • Guessing the century on two-digit years. An apostrophe date like '65 almost always means 1965, but check the print's age and style.
  • Trusting a printed camera date. The date stamped on a photo came from the camera's clock. If it was never set, the stamp can be years off.
  • Leaving the date at today. It makes a scan impossible to find by year in Photos, Google Photos or any other app.

Doing it with Heirloom

Heirloom sets the date while you scan, so you don't have to fix it afterwards.

  1. Scan your prints as usual with Scan Photos. In the editor, each photo has a Date taken card. Tap it to open When was it taken?
  2. Pick the Year. If that's all you know, turn on Only the decade is known. Otherwise choose a Month or season and a Day (each has a Not sure option), and turn on Approximate (circa) if you're guessing. The sheet shows what it will save, such as "Saved as …", and notes that Photos will show it in that year of your library.
  3. Or tap Read the back and photograph the writing on the back of the print. Heirloom reads handwritten and printed dates such as "June 1978", "Summer '82" or "Xmas 1969", plus the note that goes with them. Tap a suggestion under Found on the photo to use it, or type the date yourself.
  4. Add a Caption for who, where and what happened.
  5. Tap Save Photo (or Save 3 Photos for a page of prints). The photo goes into your Heirloom library with that date, and the library groups scans by decade. Unless you turn off Also save to Photos in Settings, it goes into Photos dated when it was taken. The date and caption are also written into the file (EXIF and IPTC) so Google Photos, Lightroom and other apps sort it correctly.

Heirloom can only add photos to your library. It can't read it, so it can't change the date of photos already in Photos. For those, use the Adjust steps above. Everything runs on your iPhone, with no account and no uploads.
