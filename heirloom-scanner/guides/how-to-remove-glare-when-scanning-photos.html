How to remove glare when scanning photos

Glossy prints and plastic album sleeves act like small mirrors. When you photograph them with a phone, a bright patch of reflected light can wash out faces and hide detail. The good news is that glare depends on the angle and the light, not on the photo itself, so you can nearly always get rid of it. Here's how to prevent it, and what to do when you can't.

Why glare happens

Glossy paper, laminate and plastic sleeves reflect light the way a mirror does. If a lamp or a window sits at the right angle, its light bounces off the print and straight into your lens. A reflection is extra light added on top of the picture. Matte prints reflect far less, which is why glare is mainly a problem with glossy ones.

The useful thing to know is this: when you move the phone, or the light, the reflection moves, but the photo underneath stays exactly where it is.

Prevent most glare before you shoot

  • Turn off the flash. It puts a bright spot right in the middle of the picture.
  • Move lamps out of the way. When you shoot straight down at a print on a table, a lamp directly overhead sits in the reflection path. Switch it off, or light the print from the side.
  • Use a window, not a bulb. A large, soft source such as a window on an overcast day gives broad, faint reflections instead of a hot spot. Keep direct sun off the print.
  • Watch your own reflection. Your phone, your arms and a white shirt can all show up in a glossy surface. Stand so you aren't in the mirror path, and wear dark clothes if you can.
  • Tilt a few degrees. Shooting slightly off straight sends the reflection somewhere else. Don't tilt much, or the print will look distorted.
  • Take loose prints out of their sleeves. If a print comes away easily, remove it from the plastic. If it's stuck, leave it alone.

When glare won't go away: take two angles

Reflections move when the camera moves. Take one photo, then move a little to the side, or tilt the phone slightly, so the bright patch lands somewhere else, and take another photo of the same page. Each shot has glare in a different place.

Because reflected light only adds brightness, the true picture at any spot is no brighter than the darker of the two shots. Keep the darker version of each spot and the glare disappears. You can do that by hand in a photo editor with two layers, but the shots must be lined up carefully, and that's slow work for a whole album.

For the best result, keep the two shots alike: the same framing, the same lighting and the same distance, with only the angle changing a little. If the page moves between photos, or you move too far, they won't line up.

Common mistakes

  • Not moving enough. If the second shot still has glare in the same area, the two shots can't cancel each other. Shift or tilt until the bright patch has clearly moved.
  • Moving too much. A big change of angle distorts the print. A small shift is enough.
  • Leaving the flash or torch on. Each shot then has a fresh reflection.
  • Casting a shadow. When you combine shots by keeping the darker version, a shadow in either photo stays in the result. Keep your hand and phone out of the light.
  • Cleaning too late. Dust and fingerprints on the sleeve catch the light. Wipe the sleeve gently with a dry microfiber cloth first.
