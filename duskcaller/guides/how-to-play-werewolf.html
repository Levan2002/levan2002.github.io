How to play Werewolf (Mafia): rules, roles and a sample night

Werewolf, also known as Mafia, is a party game of hidden roles and bluffing. A few players are secretly wolves; everyone else is a villager trying to work out who. It needs no board, only a way to hand out secret roles, and it is best with a group of six or more. This guide explains the rules, the roles you need to start, how a night and a day work, and what a typical round looks like.

The idea in one minute

Players are split into two teams. The village is the majority, and each villager only knows their own role. The wolves are a small hidden group who know each other. Each night the wolves secretly pick one villager to eliminate. Each day everyone discusses who the wolves might be, and the group votes to eliminate one player.

  • The village wins when every wolf has been eliminated.
  • The wolves win when there are as many wolves as everyone else left (for example one wolf and one villager).

Mafia is the same game with a different story: the mafia instead of wolves, a detective instead of a seer, a city instead of a village. The rules above work for both.

What you need

  • Players. Five is the practical minimum. Most groups enjoy it from about eight players up, and it can stretch to twenty or more.
  • A moderator, also called the narrator or game master. This person knows every role, runs the night, announces what happened and does not play. This is the usual sticking point: someone has to sit out.
  • A way to deal secret roles: cards, folded slips of paper, or an app.

The roles to start with

  • Werewolf. Each night the wolves wake together and agree on a victim. By day they pretend to be villagers.
  • Villager. No powers, only your wits and your vote.
  • Seer. Each night the Seer points at one player and learns whether they are on the wolves' side.
  • Doctor. Each night the Doctor protects one player from the wolves' attack. A common rule is that the Doctor can't protect the same player two nights in a row.
  • Hunter. When the Hunter is eliminated, by night or by vote, they take one other player down with them.

Once your group knows the basics, add roles one at a time: a Witch with one healing potion and one poison, Cupid who links two lovers, an Elder who survives the first attack. Don't add them all at once; every new role is another rule people must remember.

How a round works

Night

Everyone closes their eyes. The moderator wakes the roles one at a time, in the same order every night, and each role silently makes its choice. For example: "Seer, open your eyes and point at someone. Close your eyes. Doctor, open your eyes and point at the player you protect. Close your eyes. Werewolves, open your eyes and silently agree on a victim."

Dawn

Everyone opens their eyes. The moderator announces who was eliminated in the night. If the Doctor protected the wolves' victim, nobody died. Eliminated players leave the discussion. Many groups also reveal their role, which gives the village information; others keep it hidden for a harder game.

Day

The living players discuss, accuse and defend themselves. A time limit of two to three minutes keeps it lively. Then everyone votes for one player, and the player with the most votes is eliminated. If the vote is tied, groups either vote again or eliminate nobody; agree which before you start. Then night falls again, and it repeats until a team wins.

A sample game with seven players

Seven players: two wolves, a Seer, a Doctor and three villagers. On night one the Seer checks Ana and learns she is not a wolf. The Doctor protects themselves. The wolves choose Ben. At dawn, Ben is out and his role, a villager, is revealed.

In the day, Ana urges caution, Dev accuses Cleo, and Cleo defends herself loudly. The vote eliminates Cleo, who turns out to be a villager: a bad day for the village. On night two the Seer checks Dev and learns he is a wolf. The wolves target the Seer, but the Doctor guesses it and protects them, so at dawn nobody dies. Now the Seer has to decide whether to reveal what they know and risk becoming the wolves' next target. That decision is the heart of the game.

Tips for a first game

  • Start simple: wolves, a Seer, and villagers. Add the Doctor and Hunter in game two.
  • Dead players stay quiet. No facial hints, no pointing.
  • Villagers: ask questions, and watch who avoids answering. Don't vote just because someone is loud.
  • Wolves: keep your story consistent and sound like a villager: calm, curious, specific.
  • Agree the house rules first: does the first night have a kill, are roles revealed on death, what happens on a tie.

Common mistakes

  • Peeking and leaks. Eyes really closed, and nobody reacting when a role is called.
  • Skipping roles that are out. If the Seer is eliminated, the moderator should still call "Seer" and pause, otherwise everyone knows the Seer is gone.
  • A moderator who forgets. The Doctor's save from two nights ago, the Witch's used potion: it's easy to lose track.
  • Too many roles for a small table. Five or six players don't need a Witch and a Cupid.
