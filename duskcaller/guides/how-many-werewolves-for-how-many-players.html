How many werewolves for how many players?

The most common setup question in Werewolf and Mafia is how many wolves to put in the deck. Too few and the village wins every time; too many and the game is over by the second night. This guide gives you a simple starting table for any group from 5 to 20 players, explains why the numbers work, and shows how to tune the balance after a game.

The rule of thumb

A good starting point is about one wolf for every four players. Small tables lean slightly lower and bigger ones slightly higher. These are the numbers Duskcaller's presets use:

PlayersWerewolves
5 to 61
7 to 102
11 to 143
15 to 184
19 to 205

It's a starting point, not a law. Groups differ, and a table of experienced bluffers can handle more wolves than a first-time group.

Why wolves must be well under half

The wolves win when they are as many as everyone else still alive. So with 8 players and 4 wolves they would win on the spot, before the game even starts. Every night the wolves eliminate a villager and every day the village may eliminate a wolf, so the gap has to be big enough that the village has real time to find them. If you ever pick a number of wolves that is half the table or more, the game isn't playable.

Special roles: what to add and when

The wolves and villagers make the base game. Special roles give the village information or protection. A classic table adds them in this order:

  • Seer: from the smallest table. It's the village's main source of information.
  • Doctor: from about six players, so there's someone to be protected.
  • Hunter: from about eight players; a good deterrent against careless votes.

For a first game, use fewer special roles than this: wolves, a Seer and villagers is plenty. Once your group has played a few rounds, bring in extras like the Witch, Cupid, the Elder or the Jester one at a time, so people can learn what each one does to the balance.

How to tell if a setup is balanced

  • The village wins every game by night two or three: add a wolf, or take out a protective role like the Doctor.
  • The wolves win before anyone has talked much: remove a wolf, or add the Seer or the Hunter.
  • Games drag on: too many villagers with no powers. Add a role, or lower the discussion timer.
  • One role dominates: a Seer who is never killed, or a Doctor who always guesses right, can decide the game. Check the house rules for the Doctor protecting themselves or repeating a protection.

Also remember that experienced groups read each other better than new ones, so a deck that is fair for beginners may favour the village after a few weeks.

Small tables and big tables

With 5 or 6 players and one wolf, the game is quick and the lone wolf has a hard job: the village only needs to find one player. With 15 to 20 players, discussion needs structure, because not everyone can speak at once. A discussion timer and a quick vote keep a big group moving, and more wolves give the pack more room to hide.

Doing it with Duskcaller

Duskcaller builds the deck for your table size and shows you how it balances.

  1. Tap Start a night, then add your players under Players. The count shows as "of 5–20".
  2. Under The deck choose a preset. First Night is wolves and a Seer (plus a Doctor from seven players), Classic adds the Doctor and Hunter. Both are free. Moonlit and Full Moon add more roles and need Duskcaller Pro. Add or remove players, and the preset's wolf count and roles update to fit.
  3. Watch the balance meter. It reads from Werewolves favoured through Balanced to Village favoured as you change the deck.
  4. Use the minus and plus buttons next to each role to adjust the count. Villagers fill every remaining seat. If the deck is invalid, such as too many wolves, Duskcaller tells you ("Too many werewolves: they must be fewer than half the table").
  5. Tap Save and name the setup. It appears under Saved setups on the home screen for next time.

The same deck builder works for Mafia: choose Mafia city under Story and the roles become Mafioso, Detective and so on.
