How to listen to a PDF on your iPhone

A PDF looks like a printed page, and that is exactly what makes it awkward to listen to. Most read-aloud tools read the page the way the file stores it: the running title at the top of every page, the page number at the bottom, footnote markers and words broken across lines with a hyphen. On a forty-page report that adds up to a lot of noise.

Built into iOS: Speak Screen

iOS can read whatever is on screen. Turn on Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content › Speak Screen, open the PDF and swipe down with two fingers from the top of the screen. It works anywhere, but it reads every visible element, stops at page turns in some apps, and doesn’t remember where you were.

With Voxleaf

  1. Open the PDF in Voxleaf. In the library tap + › Files. From Mail or another app, use the share button on the PDF and choose Voxleaf.
  2. Let it tidy the text. Voxleaf compares the top and bottom lines of every page and drops the ones that repeat, such as the report title and “Page 3 of 40”. Hyphenated line breaks are joined, so “informa-” and “tion” come out as one word.
  3. Use the outline. If the PDF has bookmarks, they become chapters. Tap the list button at the top to jump.
  4. Press play. The sentence being read is shaded and the current word is highlighted, so you can look up at any moment and find your place.

Scanned PDFs

Some PDFs contain only pictures of pages. If most pages have no text, Voxleaf recognises the text from the images on your iPhone. It takes a few seconds per page, and the result is usually very close to the printed text. Two-column layouts are read column by column.

Tips for long documents

  • Try 1.25× or 1.5× once you are used to the voice. Most people understand speech comfortably well above their reading speed.
  • Skip back 15 seconds whenever your attention drifts; it is quicker than finding the sentence again.
  • Set the sleep timer to the end of the chapter if you listen in bed.
