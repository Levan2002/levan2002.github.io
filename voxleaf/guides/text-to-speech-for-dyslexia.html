Listening while you read: text to speech for dyslexia and ADHD

Reading with your ears and eyes at once has a name in education research: bimodal reading. Hearing the words while following them on the page takes some of the load off decoding, keeps a steady pace, and gives the mind something to hold on to when attention wanders. Many people with dyslexia or ADHD find that it lets them get through long texts they would otherwise put off.

It is not a cure or a treatment, and it doesn’t suit everyone. It is a tool, like glasses for a long drive, and it is worth trying for a week.

Settings worth trying in Voxleaf

Word highlight
On by default. The coral word helps your eyes keep up with the voice. If it feels busy, turn it off in the Aa menu and keep only the sentence shading.
Text size
Larger text with more space between lines is easier to track. The Aa menu has a size slider.
Typeface
Try each of the three. Some readers prefer the rounded sans-serif, others the book serif.
Page colour
Sepia or dark paper reduces glare for many people, especially in the evening.
Speed
Start a little slower than normal speech, then raise it as following along gets easier.

For study

  • Photograph handouts and textbook pages and listen to them on the way to class.
  • Use the 15-second skip back to re-hear a definition instead of re-reading it.
  • Long articles from the web can be imported from a link, without the adverts and menus around them.

If reading is a struggle at school or work, a teacher, an educational psychologist or your doctor can help with an assessment and support that fits you.
