How to move Quizlet flashcards to Brightdeck

By Levani Topchishvili · 6 October 2026

Quizlet does not hand you a deck file you can drop into another app, but it does let you export a set as plain text. That text is all Brightdeck needs: paste it in, tell Brightdeck how the terms and definitions are separated, and each line becomes a card. It takes a couple of minutes, and the whole thing happens on your device.

What carries over, and what does not

The export contains the text of each term and definition. Pictures that were attached to the cards on Quizlet are not part of that text, and neither is your Quizlet study history. You get fresh cards that start from zero, which is a fine place to begin because Brightdeck's scheduler learns from the ratings you give it. You can add pictures to cards afterwards.

Step 1: open the set and choose Export

  1. Open the study set you want in Quizlet, in a browser or in their app.
  2. Find the menu with the three dots near the set title and choose Export. Menus move around over time, so look for the word Export.
  3. Quizlet shows a box of text with one card per line. It also lets you choose what goes between term and definition and what goes between rows. The usual choices are a tab and a new line.
  4. Take note of the choices you make. You will repeat them in Brightdeck.

Using a tab between the term and the definition is the safest choice, because tabs almost never appear inside a card. If your definitions contain commas, avoid the comma option.

Step 2: copy the text

Use Quizlet's copy button, or select all the text in the box and copy it. If you are working on a computer and want the set on your phone, send the text to yourself in a note or message, or put it in a shared note, then copy it on the phone.

Step 3: paste it into Brightdeck

  1. Open Brightdeck, tap + on the Decks screen, choose Import Deck…, then Paste text. If the text is on your clipboard it is already in the box; otherwise paste it in.
  2. Choose the separator that matches your export: Tab, Comma, Semicolon, Dash or a custom separator you type yourself.
  3. Give the deck a name. Under the button, Brightdeck shows the first card as front → back, so you can check the split before you tap Import.

One line makes one card. The term becomes the front and the definition becomes the back.

Choosing the right separator

  • Tab: best for most sets. Match it to Quizlet's tab option.
  • Comma: fine for short single-word cards, but a comma inside any definition will split the card in the wrong place.
  • Semicolon: a good alternative when your text contains commas.
  • Dash: workable for simple pairs like word and translation, as long as neither side contains a dash.
  • Custom: pick something that never appears in your text, for example || or =>, set the same thing as Quizlet's custom separator, and enter it in Brightdeck.

Step 4: tidy up and choose card types

  1. Open the deck and scroll through a few cards. If some are split wrongly, the separator appears inside your text. Delete the deck, change the separator in Quizlet, export again and re-import.
  2. For vocabulary you want to learn in both directions, use the Both ways card type so every term is also tested from the definition side.
  3. For spelling-sensitive material, use Type the answer. Brightdeck checks your answer letter by letter, and if you are off only by case or accents it shows as "almost".
  4. For long definitions, consider turning key words into blanks with the Fill the blank type and the Make Blank button.

Step 5: set the deck up for how you study

  • Pick your target retention for the deck. The default is 90%, and you can move it anywhere from 80 to 97%. Higher means more reviews and better recall.
  • If a test is coming, set an exam date. Brightdeck caps the intervals so each card comes back before the exam, and it shows an exam readiness percentage.
  • For language decks, choose the deck's language so cards can be read aloud with the device's voices, and decide whether to auto-read.

Importing several sets

Repeat the steps for each set. Keeping one deck per Quizlet set makes it easy to give each a separate exam date or retention level. If you would rather have one big deck, paste all the text together, as long as it uses the same separator throughout.

Coming from a file instead?

If your cards are in a spreadsheet, save it as CSV or TSV and open the file in Brightdeck from the Files app or the share sheet. The same one-card-per-row idea applies.
