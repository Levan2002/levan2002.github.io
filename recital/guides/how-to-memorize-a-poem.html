How to memorize a poem

A poem is one of the easiest things to learn by heart, because its rhythm and rhyme are already memory aids. Whether it's for class, a recital, a wedding or just for yourself, here's how to learn one quickly and, more importantly, how to keep it.

Read it for meaning first

Before you try to memorize anything, read the poem a few times and ask what it's about. Look up any word you don't know. Picture the images the poet describes. A poem you understand is far easier to learn than a string of words, because each line grows out of the last.

Notice the structure

Look at how the poem is built. Is there a rhyme scheme? A steady beat? A refrain that comes back? Each of these is a free clue. When you know a line ends in a word that rhymes with the one before, you already have half of it. When a refrain repeats, you only need to learn it once, but make a note of where it differs, because that's where people slip.

Learn a stanza at a time

Don't try to swallow the whole poem. Learn the first stanza, or the first four lines, until you can say it without looking. Then learn the second. Then say both together. Keep adding stanzas, always going back to the start, so the opening is the most secure.

Say it aloud, with the rhythm

Poems are made to be heard. Say each line out loud, leaning into the beat, and your ear will help your memory. Walking while you recite works well for many people, because the rhythm of your steps matches the rhythm of the lines.

Give yourself prompts, then take them away

A good middle step between reading and reciting is to keep only the first letter of each word. Look at S I c t t a s d and see whether the line comes back. When it does, you know it; when it doesn't, you know exactly which word to work on. After that, try it with nothing at all.

Make pictures

Attach a small image to each line or stanza: a summer's day, a road in a wood, a river. Pictures tend to be easier to recall than abstract words, and the sequence of pictures will guide you through the poem.

Review it over time

This is where most people go wrong. A poem learned once and left alone fades within days. Instead, recite it again tomorrow, then in three days, then in a week, then in a month. Each review takes a minute and each one makes the memory last longer. The same method works for a verse, a prayer or a hymn.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on the look of the page. If you only remember where a line sits on the page, you won't recall it without the page. Practise from memory.
  • Rushing past punctuation. Commas and line breaks tell you where to breathe, and breathing is part of remembering.
  • Learning everything in one sitting. It feels good that evening and is mostly gone by the weekend.
  • Mixing up similar stanzas. When two stanzas start alike, learn the point where they split.

Doing it with Recital

Recital is an iPhone app for learning any text word for word, and it's a good fit for poems. Everything stays on your iPhone.

  1. Add the poem. If your library is empty, tap Try a starter text to practise with a public-domain classic. Otherwise tap Add a text, then Type or paste (or Scan pages for a printed page). Choose Poem under What is it?, optionally add the author, and tap Save.
  2. Tap Listen to hear the poem read line by line, each word lit as it's spoken.
  3. Tap Fade. Words disappear a step at a time, the ones that carry the meaning first. Tap Hide more to keep going, and tap a gap to peek.
  4. Use the menu next to Practice to work on one stanza at a time (it offers Part 1, Part 2 and so on), then switch back to Whole text.
  5. Try First Letters, where only the first letter of each word is left, and then Type Test, where you type the first letter of every word from memory. Chinese, Japanese and Korean texts get a word quiz instead.
  6. Tap Recite Aloud, then the microphone, and say the poem. Recital follows along on your iPhone and marks any word you skip or change.
  7. Let the reviews do the keeping. Recital brings each text back at spaced intervals, shows how well you know it on a five-step ladder, and can remind you daily in Settings.
