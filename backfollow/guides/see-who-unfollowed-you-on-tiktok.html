How to see who unfollowed you on TikTok

TikTok never tells you when someone unfollows you. You usually notice because your follower count drops by one. To find out who it was, you need two copies of your follower list taken at different times and a way to compare them.

What TikTok shows you (and doesn't)

TikTok shows your current followers and following lists, and it shows you new followers in your notifications. It does not keep a visible history of people who left, and there is no official "unfollowers" screen. Everything else, including any list of unfollowers, has to be worked out by comparing two snapshots.

Why the lists on your profile aren't enough

The Followers list on your profile only shows who follows you right now. If you looked at it last week and again today, you would have to remember (or have written down) the earlier names to see who is missing. For a small account that is possible, for a larger one it isn't. Followers also appear in an order that changes, so scrolling through is error-prone.

Method 1: take snapshots by hand

  1. Open your profile and tap Followers.
  2. Copy or screenshot the list, noting the date.
  3. Repeat later. Names in the old list that are missing from the new one have unfollowed you or are no longer on TikTok.

This works for a few dozen followers. Beyond that, screenshots are slow to compare, and you can miss accounts as you scroll.

Method 2: use TikTok's data download

TikTok lets you download a copy of your data, including your follower and following lists. Doing this twice, some time apart, gives you two proper snapshots.

  1. In TikTok, open your profile and tap the menu (☰), then Settings and privacy.
  2. Tap Account, then Download your data.
  3. On the Request data tab, choose a file format. JSON or TXT both work with Backfollow.
  4. Tap Request data. TikTok prepares the file, which can take a few days.
  5. Check back on the Download data tab, download the file and save it to your iPhone. The link expires, so don't wait long.

TikTok's menus and the layout of the file can change between versions and regions. If a label differs, look for Download your data under Account. The export contains much more than followers, because it covers your whole account, so the file is larger than Instagram's follower-only export.

Once you have two exports, you can compare the follower sections yourself: copy the usernames from each file into a spreadsheet and look for names in the older list that are missing from the newer one. That works, but it takes some care.

Method 3: let Backfollow compare the exports

Backfollow reads the TikTok export on your iPhone and keeps the result. The first import gives you your current lists. Each later import is compared with the previous one, and Backfollow shows:

  • who unfollowed you since the last import
  • who followed you since then
  • who you follow that doesn't follow you back, and your mutuals

There is no login, nothing is uploaded, and it doesn't connect to your TikTok account. History between imports is part of Backfollow Pro; counts and the first 10 names of each list are free. The help page repeats the export steps.

What to expect from the results

  • You need two exports. The first import has nothing to compare against. History starts from the second one.
  • Changes are grouped by import. If three people left between your two exports, you'll see them as a group. You can't tell on which day each one left.
  • The exact date isn't always available. Following dates depend on what TikTok includes in the file.
  • Fewer followers does not always mean unfollows. Deleted, banned and deactivated accounts also disappear from your list. Backfollow can show who is missing, but it can't know why.
  • Some lists may be smaller than what you see in the app because the export is a snapshot from when you requested it.

A sensible routine

Because the TikTok export takes days, request a new one on a schedule you will actually keep, for example monthly, rather than whenever you get curious. Request the next one right after you import the previous file so you always have a recent baseline. When you have your results, decide what to do with them: unfollowing back is optional, and it is better to do it by hand than with a tool that logs in as you.

Is it safe to give an app my TikTok password?

You don't need to, and there is no reason to. The export contains what is needed, and an app that reads it can't change anything on your account. Apps that ask for your login can act as you, so it is worth being cautious about them.
