How to download your Instagram followers and following list (JSON)

Instagram will give you a copy of your followers and following lists as a file, free, from inside the app. Asking for only that part of your data is quick. This guide covers the exact steps, what is in the files and how to open them on an iPhone.

Before you start

You need the Instagram app (or instagram.com) signed in to the account you want to check. If you have more than one account, you request each account's export separately. Menu names change from time to time. The labels below are the ones used at the time of writing, so if yours differ slightly, look for the nearest match.

Request the export in the Instagram app

  1. Open Instagram, go to your profile, tap the menu (☰) and open Accounts Center.
  2. Tap Your information and permissions, then Download your information.
  3. Tap Download or transfer information and choose the account you want.
  4. Choose Some of your information. Under the Connections group, select Followers and following.
  5. Choose Download to device.
  6. Set the date range to All time. A shorter range can leave out older followers.
  7. Set the format to JSON. HTML works too, but JSON is easier for programs to read and is the format Backfollow recommends.
  8. Tap Create files and confirm.

If Instagram asks you to re-enter your password at this point, that is Instagram verifying it is you, which is normal. Only ever type your password into Instagram's own app or website.

How long does it take?

Selecting only Followers and following makes the request small, so it is usually ready within minutes to a few hours. A request for all your information, with photos and messages, can take much longer. When it is ready, Instagram sends a notification and an email with a download link. The link only works for a limited time, so download the file soon after it arrives.

Download it on your iPhone

Open the email or notification on the iPhone, tap the download link and sign in if asked. Safari downloads a ZIP file, which you can find in the Files app under Downloads. You don't have to unzip it: tap the ZIP in Files, then Share → Backfollow, or open Backfollow and tap Import and pick the file.

What is inside the export?

Unzipped, the export contains a folder for your connections with files such as these (names can vary by version):

  • followers_1.json (and followers_2.json and so on for large accounts): the accounts that follow you.
  • following.json: the accounts you follow.
  • pending_follow_requests.json: requests you sent that haven't been accepted.
  • recently_unfollowed_profiles.json: accounts you unfollowed recently.
  • close_friends.json: your Close Friends list.

Each entry has a username, a link to the profile and a timestamp. The timestamp is a Unix time (seconds since 1 January 1970) for when the follow happened, which is where "following since" dates come from. The exact structure has changed over the years, which is one reason to let an app handle the reading.

JSON or HTML?

Both contain the same information. HTML is easier to open in a browser and read by eye, but it is awkward to work with. JSON is better when a program or a spreadsheet import is going to read it. If you only plan to scroll through the names yourself, either is fine. If you plan to use Backfollow or compare exports over time, choose JSON.

Options for using the files

  1. Read them yourself. Open the HTML version in a browser, or the JSON in any text editor. Works for a quick look, but you still have to compare followers against following manually.
  2. Import into a spreadsheet. Tools such as Google Sheets or Excel can import JSON, usually with a converter or Power Query. Once the usernames are in columns, you can compare lists with formulas.
  3. Open the ZIP in Backfollow. It reads the files directly on your iPhone and shows the lists, with no conversion. A second export later lets it show who unfollowed you and new followers.

Common problems

  • The list looks shorter than expected. Check that the date range was All time. Remember also that the export is a snapshot from when it was created, and deactivated accounts may be missing.
  • I can't find the Download your information option. It is under Accounts Center. On instagram.com, open Settings and look for Accounts Center in the sidebar. If you only see account-level settings, update the app to the latest version.
  • The link has expired. Request the export again. It is quick when you select only Followers and following.
  • The file won't open on my iPhone. Try the Files app: long-press the ZIP and choose Uncompress to get a folder, and look for the followers and following files inside.

Keep it private

The export is a list of real people and your connections to them. Treat it like any personal file: don't post it, and delete it from Downloads when you no longer need it. Backfollow keeps its own copy on your device only, and you can remove it with Settings → Delete all data.
