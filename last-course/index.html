The evening, course by course

Everything a host needs, in one phone

No printing, no booklets, no one stuck reading the rules aloud. Last Course runs the game so you can enjoy your own party.

i.

Cast and invite

Assign a character to each guest and send their invitation as an image or PDF through Messages or AirDrop. Each one carries the role, costume ideas and a sealed secret brief for that guest’s eyes only.

ii.

A narrated night

The phone guides the table through four acts, from the discovery to the final accusation. Read the story aloud yourself or let the optional on-device narrator do it.

iii.

Clues and private notes

New evidence arrives act by act. Between acts the phone passes round the table so each guest can read a private note meant only for them.

iv.

The victim’s phone

Crack the lock-screen passcode as a table, then dig in: texts with recovered deleted messages, photos with details worth a closer look, the call log, a final voicemail and the notes app.

v.

Secret accusations

Each guest names a suspect, a motive and a method in private. Nobody sees anyone else’s answer until the reveal.

vi.

The truth, and the awards

The solution is revealed with a “how you could have known” walkthrough of every clue. Then hand out shareable award cards to celebrate the best performances of the night.

The hosting guide

How to host a murder mystery dinner party

A murder mystery dinner is the rare party where the guests entertain each other. Here is how to plan one, step by step, whether or not you use our app.

  1. Choose a case that suits your crowd

    Start with the mood you want. A first-time group does best with a short, easy case and simple costumes, which is why our starter case is set at a restaurant opening and runs about an hour and a half. For a seasonal party, match the theme to the date: a gothic masquerade for Halloween, a ski chalet for New Year’s Eve, a jazz-age gala for a birthday or summer evening.

    Think about your guests too. Groups who love dressing up will enjoy a costume-heavy theme. Groups who love puzzles will enjoy a harder case with more red herrings.

  2. Settle the guest count

    Six to eight guests is the sweet spot for a first party: everyone gets plenty of time in the spotlight and conversation stays around one table. Ten to twelve feels like a real gala, with more alibis to untangle.

    • Every Last Course case has six core characters who must be cast. Extra characters slot in for larger groups.
    • Invite one or two more people than you need. Someone always cancels, and the extras are optional.
    • Give your most outgoing friends the loudest roles and your quieter friends the ones with juicy secrets.

  3. Send invitations one to two weeks ahead

    One to two weeks is the right lead time. It is long enough to find a costume and short enough that nobody forgets who they are. Each invitation should include the date and time, the character’s name and public description, costume ideas, and a private brief: what this character knows, what they want and what they are hiding.

    In Last Course you cast each guest and share their invitation as an image or PDF through Messages, AirDrop or any app on the share sheet. Ask guests to keep their brief to themselves, even from their partner.

  4. Encourage costumes, but keep them easy

    Costumes do half the work of getting people into character. Suggest pieces people already own: a scarf, a blazer, pearls, a knit sweater, a mask. One strong prop, like a notebook for the critic or a camera for the photographer, is worth more than a rented outfit. Tell guests that effort counts more than expense, then reward it with a best-costume award at the end.

  5. Plan a menu that runs alongside the acts

    The easiest rhythm is one act per course: arrivals and the prologue with drinks and nibbles, the first act with a starter, the main investigation over the main course, and the accusations and reveal over dessert. Choose dishes you can make ahead, like braises, tarts, roasts and chilled desserts, so you are never stuck in the kitchen while the clues are being read. Offer a signature drink with a name from the story, with an alcohol-free version as well.

  6. Set the scene

    Dim the lights and light candles. Print or write place cards with each character’s name, and play quiet music that suits the era. Put a notepad and pen at every seat. Small touches, like a “crime scene” card on the sideboard or a locked box on the mantel, make the room feel like part of the story.

  7. Run the acts

    Open with the prologue: the setting, the victim and the moment everything went wrong. In each act, read the new clues aloud or let the narrator do it, then give everyone time to question each other in character. Guests may bend the truth to protect their secrets, but they should never lie about the one thing their brief says they must admit.

    In Last Course the phone handles the pacing. It narrates each act, reveals the clues, passes round the table for private notes and, at the right moment, opens the victim’s phone for everyone to search together. Your job is to keep the wine poured and the questions flowing.

  8. Accuse in secret, then reveal the truth

    Before the reveal, every guest makes a private accusation: who did it, why and how. Secret ballots keep it fair, because nobody can copy the best detective at the table. Then gather everyone for the reveal. The best reveals don’t just name the killer; they walk back through the evening and show which clues pointed the right way. Last Course ends every case with a “how you could have known” walkthrough for exactly this reason.

  9. Finish with awards

    End on a high with a few light-hearted awards: best detective, most convincing liar, best costume and best performance. They give quieter guests a moment to shine and give everyone a photo to share. Last Course ends every case with themed award cards you can share.

Halloween

Halloween murder mystery party ideas

Every All Hallows’ Eve for twenty years, the horror novelist Isadora Crane has opened the iron gates of Hollowmere Hall for a masquerade. This year the storm came early, the causeway is flooding, and she has promised to reveal the true ending of her unfinished novel at midnight. At one minute to midnight, a scream rings out from the locked library.

The case: Midnight at Hollowmere, 6–12 guests, about two and a half hours. Gothic atmosphere, a séance, a family legend, and a solution that is entirely human.

Costume ideas: masks are a must. Beyond that, think Victorian mourning dress, lace gloves, velvet jackets, capes, vampire glamour, a veil, or simply all black with dramatic makeup.

Decor: candles in mismatched holders, dark tablecloths, dried flowers, old books stacked on the table, a few feathers and a raven or two. Turn the overhead lights off and let the candles do the work.

To begin

  • Black olive tapenade on crostiniwith roasted red pepper
  • Roasted pumpkin soupsage brown butter

Main course

  • Red-wine braised short ribswith creamy mash, made the day before
  • Wild mushroom and chestnut piethe vegetarian choice

Dessert

  • Dark chocolate tartwith blackberries

To drink

  • “The Second Bride”blackberry and lemon, with or without gin

Roaring Twenties

1920s murder mystery party ideas

Every midsummer, a mysterious millionaire throws the most talked-about party on the coast: The Gilded Hour at Halcyon House, a waterfront mansion with a jazz band, a champagne tower and a speakeasy hidden behind a bookcase. The guests are modern people who dress the part of the Roaring Twenties. Tonight the host has promised a toast that will change everything.

The case: The Gilded Hour, 6–12 guests, about two and a half hours. Our trickiest case, set in the present day so the victim’s phone is in play.

Costume ideas: flapper dresses, beaded headbands, long pearls, feather boas, three-piece suits, suspenders, bow ties and fedoras. A long cigarette holder makes a fine prop, unlit.

Decor: gold and black, art deco shapes, fairy lights, a tower of coupe glasses, and a jazz playlist from the era. Hide the bar behind a curtain and give guests the password at the door.

  • Waldorf saladapple, celery and walnut

Main course

  • Steak with peppercorn sauceand crisp potatoes
  • Lobster or mushroom thermidora showpiece for the table

Dessert

  • Champagne jelly or a lemon chiffon pie

To drink

  • French 75 and a gin rickeysparkling lemonade for the alcohol-free version

New Year’s Eve

New Year’s Eve murder mystery party ideas

Kessler Lodge sits alone high above the Alpine village of Sankt Aurel. Every New Year’s Eve its owner, a downhill champion turned resort tycoon, invites a handful of people for fondue by the fire and fireworks over the valley. This year a blizzard closes the cable car at six, and nobody is leaving until morning. Midnight comes. The countdown comes. The host doesn’t.

The case: Snowbound at Kessler Peak, 6–12 guests, about two and a half hours. A natural fit for a party that runs up to midnight.

Costume ideas: retro ski suits, chunky knit sweaters, faux-fur hats, goggles on the forehead, and a little New Year’s glitter.

Decor: blankets and candles, pine branches, white fairy lights, and a countdown clock on the wall. Time the reveal for just after midnight.

To begin

  • Cheese fonduewith bread, potatoes and pickles

Main course

  • Raclette or a slow-roasted pork shoulderwith mountain herbs
  • Spätzle with caramelised onionsthe vegetarian choice

Dessert

  • Apple strudelwith vanilla sauce

To drink

  • Mulled wine and hot chocolatethen sparkling wine at midnight

Dinner and restaurant theme

Restaurant murder mystery dinner ideas

It is the most talked-about table in the city. Marrow, the new restaurant from television chef Julian Sorrel, opens tonight in an old butcher’s hall: one long counter, twelve seats, nine courses. He has invited the critic who could close him, the sous-chef who cooks his food, the investor who paid for the knives and more. He called the final course “The Last Course.” He never made it to dessert.

The case: Murder on the Tasting Menu, 6–8 guests, about 95 minutes. Free with the app, and the easiest way to learn the format.

Costume ideas: chef whites or an apron, a critic’s scarf and notebook, a sharp blazer for the investor, influencer sparkle, or your best opening-night outfit.

Decor: a long table, white linen, small handwritten menus at each seat, and a “reserved” card for the chef’s empty chair.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How many guests do you need?

Six to twelve. The free starter case, Murder on the Tasting Menu, is written for 6–8 guests. Midnight at Hollowmere, The Gilded Hour and Snowbound at Kessler Peak each take 6–12. Every case has six core characters who are always cast, plus optional extras for bigger groups.

How long does it take?

The starter case runs about 95 minutes. The three larger cases run about two and a half hours. Most hosts play the acts between courses, so the game and the dinner finish together. Allow a little extra time for arrivals and the awards.

Do guests need the app?

No. Only the host needs Last Course. Guests receive their invitation as an image or PDF through Messages, AirDrop or any app on the share sheet. On the night, one phone is passed around the table for private notes and secret votes.

Can the host play too?

Yes. The solution stays hidden from the host as well, so you can cast yourself as a character and solve the case with everyone else. Just don’t peek at the other guests’ secret briefs before the night.

Is it kid-friendly?

Last Course is written for teens and adults, ages 12 and up. The murders happen off stage and are never described in graphic detail, and the tone is witty and theatrical rather than scary. Some cases mention wine, champagne or cocktails as part of the setting; serve whatever drinks suit your table.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once a case is in the app, the whole evening runs on your phone without a connection, including the optional narration, which uses Apple’s on-device voices. You only need the internet to buy or restore purchases, and to send invitations by message.

How far ahead should I send invitations?

One to two weeks before the party. That gives guests time to read their character, find a costume and settle into the role.

Which languages does the app support?

The app is available in English, German, French, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Italian and Japanese.

How much does it cost?

The app and the starter case are free. Each additional case is $9.99, once. Host Pass is $29.99 a year and includes every case and new ones as they arrive. Every Case Forever is $49.99, once, for every case permanently.
