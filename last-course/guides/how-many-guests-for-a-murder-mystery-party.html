How many guests do you need for a murder mystery party?

Short answer: six is the minimum, six to eight is the sweet spot for a first party, and ten to twelve makes a proper gala. Here is why those numbers work, and what to do if your guest list doesn’t land neatly on one.

Why six is the floor

A murder mystery needs suspects. With fewer than six people at the table there are too few motives to weigh, too few secrets to trade and too few alibis to pick apart, and the killer is easy to spot by elimination.

It also helps to think in characters rather than people. A well-built case has a fixed core of characters who must be present for the puzzle to work, and extra characters who add colour and red herrings when the group is bigger. If a core character is missing, a clue can point at nobody.

Six to eight: the best first party

For a first murder mystery, aim for six to eight. Everyone gets real time in the spotlight, the whole table can hear one conversation, and you only have to keep track of a handful of secrets. It also fits around one dining table without a second one, which matters more than you would think: a game that splits into two conversations is hard to follow.

Smaller groups also play faster, which suits a weeknight.

Ten to twelve: when it feels like a gala

A bigger table is more fun in some ways. There are more alibis to untangle, more costumes in the room and more chances for unexpected alliances. The trade-offs are real, though:

  • More cross-talk. Seat people so the conversation flows, and ask guests to take turns when the table gets loud.
  • Quieter guests get lost. Give shy friends characters with a clear, specific secret, so they have something to say when the questions come.
  • Longer evenings. More people means more private notes to pass round and more accusations to hear. Plan the schedule with that in mind.
  • More food. A cold starter and a make-ahead main keep you out of the kitchen.

Beyond twelve, a single shared conversation gets hard to follow. With a larger crowd, consider running two separate tables.

Does the host count?

That’s up to you. Hosting and playing at the same time works well if the case keeps the solution hidden from you too, so you can join in and be as surprised as everyone else. Hosting without playing means you can run the timing and keep the wine flowing, but you will miss out on the guessing. If you do play, count yourself in the headcount and don’t read your guests’ secret briefs beforehand.

What if people drop out?

They will. A few ways to cope:

  • Invite one or two extra guests. If everyone comes, the extra characters are a bonus, not a problem.
  • Cast the core characters first. Give the must-have roles to your most reliable friends, and the optional ones to people who are less sure.
  • Rework the cast when numbers change. Don’t try to play two characters yourself unless you have to; a fresh casting of the smaller group is cleaner.
  • Couples: splitting them across characters usually produces the best scenes, but ask them how they feel about it first.

How long will it take?

Allow roughly an hour and a half for a shorter case and two and a half hours for a longer one, plus time for arrivals, drinks and a few awards at the end. If you serve a three-course dinner alongside the game, play one act per course so that the food and the story finish together.

Doing it with Last Course

Last Course cases are written for six to twelve guests. Every case has six core characters who are always cast, and optional extra characters for bigger groups. The free starter case, Murder on the Tasting Menu, is written for 6 to 8 guests and runs about 95 minutes. Midnight at Hollowmere, The Gilded Hour and Snowbound at Kessler Peak each take 6 to 12 guests and run about two and a half hours.

  1. On the home screen, each case card shows its guest range and running time, for example “6–8 guests” and the length of the evening. Pick the one that fits your headcount.
  2. Tap Plan a party and add each guest under Add a guest. Until you reach the minimum, the note under the list tells you how many more you need (it ends “Add 2 more”, for example). When you reach the maximum it reads “A full table: every character is in play.”
  3. The app casts everyone, you included if you’re playing. Tap a guest to swap their character, or tap Shuffle to recast the table.
  4. If someone drops out, swipe their name away and the app recasts the remaining guests. Invitations are sent from the same screen, so you can send an updated one.
  5. Once every guest has a character, Begin the evening becomes available.

Only the host needs the app. Guests receive an image or PDF invitation, and on the night one phone is passed around the table.
