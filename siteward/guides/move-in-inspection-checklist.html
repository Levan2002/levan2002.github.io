Move-in inspection checklist for renters: what to document

A move-in inspection is a record of what a rental looks like on the day you get the keys. Done well, it protects you from being charged for damage that was already there, and it protects the landlord too. This guide gives you a checklist for every room, tells you what to photograph, and shows how to end up with a signed report both sides can keep.

Why a move-in inspection matters

When a tenancy ends, the landlord compares the home with how it started. If nobody wrote down the stain on the bedroom carpet or the crack in the bathroom tile at the beginning, it can become an argument about who caused it. A dated list with photos settles that quickly.

Rules differ a lot between countries, states and provinces: some places require a landlord to give you a condition form, set a deadline for reporting problems, or limit what can be taken from a deposit. Check your local tenancy rules and your lease for the exact requirements. The checklist below is useful everywhere.

When to do it

Do it before you move your belongings in, ideally on the day you get the keys, while the home is empty and every wall and floor is visible. If you can only inspect after moving in, do it as soon as possible and note the date. If your lease or local rules give you a fixed window to report problems, put that date in your calendar.

The checklist, room by room

In every room

  • Walls: marks, scuffs, holes, damp patches, peeling paint.
  • Ceiling: stains, cracks, mould.
  • Floor: stains, burns, scratches, loose boards or tiles, worn carpet.
  • Doors: they open, close and lock; check handles, hinges and marks.
  • Windows: open and lock, glass intact, seals and blinds in order.
  • Lights and switches: every light comes on, every switch and cover plate is intact.
  • Sockets: present, secure and working. Plug in a phone charger to check.

Entrance

  • Front door and lock work; count and test every key.
  • Doorbell or intercom works.

Living room and bedrooms

  • Heating or radiators work and don't leak.
  • Wardrobes: doors, rails and shelves intact.

Kitchen

  • Cabinets and worktops: chips, burns, doors that hang crooked.
  • Sink and taps: run hot and cold, check for drips under the sink.
  • Appliances: oven, hob, fridge, extractor and anything else that's included; note brand and condition.

Bathroom

  • Toilet flushes and doesn't rock; check around the base for leaks.
  • Bath or shower, basin and taps: run them and check the drains.
  • Tiles, grout and sealant: cracks, mould, gaps.
  • Ventilation: the extractor fan or window works.

Safety and meters

  • Smoke alarms present and responding to their test button. Check for a carbon monoxide alarm where gas appliances are fitted.
  • Meter readings for electricity, gas and water, each with a photo of the meter.
  • Keys, access cards and fobs handed over, with a count.

How to photograph a rental

  • Take a wide photo of each room first, then close-ups of every flaw.
  • Put something in the frame for scale, such as a coin or a tape measure, for small marks.
  • Keep the photos date-stamped by taking them with your phone camera and saving them together with the report.
  • Describe each flaw in words as well: "Bedroom, wall left of the window: 10 cm scuff at hand height." Photos show the flaw; the note says exactly where.

Finish with a signed record

Ask the landlord or agent to look at your list, agree it and sign it. If they won't, send it to them by email, so there is a dated record they received it. Keep a copy yourself. At move-out, do the same inspection again and compare. Normal wear from living in a home is treated differently from damage in many places, so keep your wording factual and don't exaggerate.

Doing it with Siteward

Siteward has a rental template with the rooms, ratings, meters and signatures built in.

  1. On Projects tap New project and choose Move-in / move-out inspection ("Condition report for landlords and tenants").
  2. Enter the property name and address, the tenant or landlord under Client, your name as inspector and the Inspection date. Set Inspection type to Move-in. Tap Create.
  3. Open the Rooms tab. Each room has checks for walls, ceiling, floor, doors, windows, lights and switches, sockets, plus room-specific ones like cabinets and sink in the kitchen. Tap a rating for each: New, Good, Fair, Poor or N/A.
  4. For a problem, tap the row to add a note, or tap Add as an item with photos to make it a defect with photos and markup. It then appears in the Defects tab.
  5. Open Meters & keys and enter the electricity, gas and water meter readings, and the keys and fobs handed over.
  6. Tap Report. In Options keep Room-by-room condition, Meters & keys and Sign-off page switched on. In Signatures, hand the phone over so the tenant and landlord can each sign with a finger. Then send the Property Condition Report as a PDF with Email, Share or Files.

When you move out, make a second project, choose the same template and set Inspection type to Move-out, so you have both reports to compare. Siteward records condition; it isn't legal advice, and what a deposit can cover is a matter for your local rules and lease.
